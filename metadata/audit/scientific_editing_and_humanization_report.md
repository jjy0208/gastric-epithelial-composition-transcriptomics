# Scientific editing and humanization report

## Scope

The full English manuscript was edited for scientific logic, reviewer readability and a more natural authorial voice. Numerical results, thresholds, sample sizes, dataset identifiers, statistical tests, reference numbering and the strength of the scientific claims were preserved.

## Problems identified

- The opening of the Discussion repeated the Results before answering the central biological question.
- Several paragraphs relied on formulaic transitions, including stock conclusion phrases and mechanically numbered limitations.
- Long passive sentences in the Methods obscured the actor, unit of analysis and order of operations.
- Repeated contrast formulas made the prose sound generated rather than written for the specific study.
- Some claims appeared before their evidential boundary, forcing a reviewer to read ahead to find the qualification.

## Editorial changes

- The Introduction now ends with one explicit research question and the validation design that addresses it.
- Results paragraphs lead with the finding and then give the estimate, uncertainty and interpretive limit.
- The Discussion now answers the central question first: the bulk signal has a strong compositional component, while the within-epithelial component differs between cohorts.
- Conflicting single-cell results are discussed as heterogeneity to be explained, not averaged into a universal effect.
- Spatial evidence is separated clearly into what it establishes (localization) and what it cannot establish (a paired tissue contrast or protein-level validation).
- Limitations are grouped by inferential layer instead of presented as a template-like numbered list.
- Methods sentences now identify what was analyzed, how pairs were defined, which annotations were retained and where patient-level inference was used.
- Formulaic expressions, unnecessary intensifiers and em or en dashes were removed from article prose. Bibliographic punctuation was retained in the reference list to preserve citation metadata.

## Scientific invariants

The edit did not alter:

- the 73-gene candidate definition;
- any cohort, patient, sample, cell, spot or section count;
- any fold change, coefficient, correlation, confidence interval, P value, FDR or heterogeneity estimate;
- the prespecified 20-cell threshold or WGCNA criteria;
- the distinction between descriptive composition analysis and causal mediation;
- the conclusion that the within-epithelial effect is cohort-dependent;
- the absence of perturbation, protein-level validation and independent malignant-cell calls.

## Second-pass humanization check

The revised prose was screened for recurring AI-style signals: generic opening claims, repetitive signposting, symmetrical contrast templates, exaggerated certainty, stacked caveats, vague actors and abrupt conclusion formulas. Remaining technical repetition was retained only where needed for reproducibility or consistent terminology.
